    Results on families in progress

    Latest numbers I logged for each family. Pending variants are not shown. Two of the three have strong Sharpe and still fail, and the table shows why.

    Fitness grows with the square root of returns per unit of turnover. Moving from 0.55 to 1.0 at the same Sharpe needs about 3.3 times the return per unit of turnover. That gap is the work.

    Sub-universe cutoffs depend on the signal, so that column shows the value without a verdict. Fitness gap is 1 divided by fitness squared: the factor on return per unit of turnover needed to reach 1.0 with Sharpe unchanged.

    Why fitness is hard to move

    Fitness equals Sharpe times the square root of absolute returns divided by turnover, with turnover floored at 12.5%. The shaded region is every returns and turnover pair that clears 1.0 at the chosen Sharpe, inside the 1% to 70% turnover window.

    Two levers only: earn more per trade, or trade less. Below 12.5% turnover the floor stops further reward.

    Defaults reproduce Sharpe 1.33 and Fitness 0.55 from the volume-price family. Returns and turnover here are illustrative.

    Why peer-group transforms work

    A raw signal often carries a subindustry bet. Here the four groups have different average signal levels, as real fundamentals do. Switch the operator and watch the net exposure of each group.

    group_rank and group_zscore compare each stock with its own subindustry peers, so the book keeps stock selection and drops the sector bet. This matches what I found: peer-group comparisons generally beat market-wide normalization.

    Subindustry ABCD

    48 synthetic stocks, 12 per subindustry. Weights are demeaned across the universe and scaled to gross exposure of 100%. Chips show each group net weight.

    What I have learned so far

    • Outer wrappers hurt. Wrapping a signal in ts_rank or ts_mean as the outermost layer tends to kill Sharpe.
    • Decay has a ceiling. For volume-correlation signals, decay beyond 5 or 6 pushed Sharpe under the threshold.
    • Peers beat the market. group_zscore and group_rank by subindustry generally outperform market-wide normalization.
    • Volatile signals work better as filters. The buzz signal reached Sharpe 1.54 as a conditional filter, then needed turnover control.
    • Stacked transforms degrade. Heavy group_rank layers and stacked smoothing hurt signals that work closer to raw form.
